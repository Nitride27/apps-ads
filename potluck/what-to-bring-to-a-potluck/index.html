What to bring to a potluck

Bring something that travels well, serves 8 to 12 and needs no oven at the host's: a pasta bake, a big salad, a slow-cooker dish, a tray of brownies or a cooler of drinks. Check the sign-up sheet first so you fill a gap, not double one.

Easy ideas by course

  • Mains: baked ziti, chili, pulled pork in a slow cooker, enchiladas, a whole roast chicken
  • Sides: mac and cheese, roasted vegetables, potato salad, coleslaw, rice pilaf
  • Salads: Caesar, Greek, pasta salad, a grain bowl that holds for hours
  • Starters: deviled eggs, cheese board, spinach dip, fruit platter, veggie tray
  • Desserts: brownies, cookies, sheet cake, pie, lemon bars
  • Drinks and supplies: sparkling water, juice, ice, plates, cups, serving spoons

Ask the host first

Ask about allergies, whether there is fridge or oven space, and roughly how many are coming. Label your dish with what is in it.

Questions

What is the easiest thing to bring to a potluck?

Drinks, ice, or plates and cups. They are always needed and easy to forget.

How much food should I bring to a potluck?

Enough for about half the guests if every guest brings a dish. A 9×13 pan serves 12 to 15 as a side.
